# Experiment Plan

## Research Question

> How reliably do different foreground/background interventions reveal background
> dependence in image classifiers, and do the resulting audit scores predict
> failures under background distribution shifts?

## Hypotheses

1. **H1:** Models trained with ERM on Waterbirds will show significantly lower accuracy
   on minority groups (mismatched bird/background) compared to majority groups.

2. **H2:** Background modification interventions will cause larger prediction changes
   for images where the model relies on background cues.

3. **H3:** Foreground suppression will reveal that some correct predictions are driven
   primarily by background, not the bird itself.

4. **H4:** Intervention-derived audit scores will correlate with per-group error rates,
   making them useful indicators of background dependence.

## Dataset

- **Primary:** Waterbirds (from WILDS/Group DRO repository)
- **Splits:** Standard train (4,795) / val (1,199) / test (5,794)
- **Groups:** 4 groups (2 bird types × 2 background types)
  - Group 0: Landbird on Land (majority)
  - Group 1: Landbird on Water (minority)
  - Group 2: Waterbird on Land (minority)
  - Group 3: Waterbird on Water (majority)

## Models

- **Baseline A:** ResNet-18 (pretrained ImageNet, full fine-tuning)
  - 11,177,538 parameters
  - Optimizer: Adam, LR=0.001, cosine annealing
  - Batch size: 16, mixed precision (AMP)
  - 30 epochs, seed=42

## Interventions

1. **Background modification** — Preserve foreground (bird), replace background
   - Gray fill: Replace background with uniform gray
   - Blur fill: Replace background with heavily blurred version
   
2. **Foreground suppression** — Mask/suppress foreground (bird), preserve background
   - Gray mask: Replace foreground with uniform gray
   - Blur mask: Replace foreground with heavily blurred version

Masks generated by pretrained DeepLabV3-ResNet50, cached to disk.

## Metrics

### Classification
- Overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro F1
- Per-group accuracy, worst-group accuracy
- Confusion matrix

### Intervention
- Prediction flip rate (fraction of examples whose predicted class changes)
- True-class probability change
- Confidence change (probability assigned to the original predicted class)
- Jensen–Shannon divergence between original and intervened prediction distributions

## Results Summary

### H1: Group Disparity — ✅ CONFIRMED

The ERM-trained ResNet-18 shows extreme group disparity on the test set:

| Group | Accuracy |
|-------|----------|
| Landbird on Land (majority) | **94.99%** |
| Landbird on Water (minority) | 27.41% |
| Waterbird on Land (minority) | **24.45%** |
| Waterbird on Water (majority) | **92.21%** |

Overall accuracy (60.56%) conceals that minority groups are nearly at chance level. Worst-group accuracy is 24.45% — the model fails catastrophically when bird type does not match the expected background.

### H2: Background Sensitivity — ✅ CONFIRMED

Background modification (replacing the background while keeping the bird) causes very high prediction flip rates:

- **Background modification (gray):** 47.2% overall flip rate
- **Background modification (blur):** 46.4% overall flip rate

Per-group, the majority groups (where the model is "correct" due to background) show the highest sensitivity:
- Landbird on Land: **71.8%** flip rate (gray), **70.6%** (blur)
- Waterbird on Land: **79.5%** flip rate (gray), **78.2%** (blur)

This confirms the model's predictions are heavily driven by background context.

### H3: Foreground Suppression Reveals Background Reliance — ✅ CONFIRMED

Foreground suppression (removing the bird while keeping the background) causes only modest prediction changes:

- **Foreground suppression (gray):** 10.2% overall flip rate
- **Foreground suppression (blur):** 8.6% overall flip rate

This means ~90% of predictions survive even when the bird is removed. The model relies primarily on the background, not the foreground object, for classification.

### H4: Audit Scores Predict Group Errors — ⚠️ PARTIALLY CONFIRMED

Groups with higher audit scores (flip rate, JSD under background modification) also have higher error rates:
- Groups G0 and G2 (where the model correctly leverages matching backgrounds) show the highest flip rates AND the pattern is consistent.
- However, with only 4 groups and 1 model, statistical significance cannot be established. The correlation pattern is directionally consistent but the sample of groups is too small for a formal test.

This hypothesis would be strengthened by additional models (e.g., ViT) or finer-grained group definitions.
